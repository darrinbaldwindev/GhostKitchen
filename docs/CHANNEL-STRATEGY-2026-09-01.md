# Ghost Kitchen Channel Strategy

**Status:** Working strategic direction — validation required  
**Date:** 2026-09-01

## Core finding

Digital ordering should be treated as a core capability, while third-party marketplaces should be treated primarily as acquisition/reach channels rather than the only customer relationship.

FSANZ reports 32.4% of surveyed Australians ordered restaurant/take-away meals or drinks online in the preceding 30 days. Roy Morgan reports 82.5% of Australians aged 14+ ate takeaway food from fast-food outlets in an average six months in the year to June 2026.

## Proposed channel architecture

### 1. Marketplace acquisition

Use third-party marketplaces where their customer reach can produce profitable incremental demand. Model commission, delivery and promotional costs explicitly.

### 2. Direct ordering

Build a first-party ordering relationship capable of capturing customer consent, preferences, order history and retention opportunities.

### 3. Hybrid fulfilment

Allow the business to choose the lowest-risk delivery fulfilment path by order and geography, subject to food-safety and service standards.

### 4. Pickup

Treat pickup as an important margin-preserving option where the pilot location supports it.

## Strategic rule

Do not optimise solely for gross marketplace sales. Optimise for contribution per order, repeat purchase, customer ownership and operational reliability.

## Free delivery

Free delivery remains a customer-offer question, not a cost assumption. The economics model must compare threshold, partial subsidy, full subsidy and customer-funded structures.

## Evidence status

Current evidence supports continued investment in digital ordering and a hybrid channel model. It does not establish the final platform mix, delivery radius, pricing or customer acquisition budget.
