# Ghost Kitchen Pilot Evidence Base

**Date:** 2026-08-31  
**Status:** Working evidence pack — validation required  
**Purpose:** Establish evidence inputs for pilot concept selection and the Model B unit-economics work without prematurely selecting a franchise concept.

## 1. Market / channel evidence

- Food Standards Australia New Zealand's 2025 online-shopping research reports that 32.4% of surveyed Australians ordered restaurant/take-away meals or drinks online in the preceding 30 days. This supports treating digital ordering as a core channel assumption, but it is not a forecast for a specific Ghost Kitchen location. Source: FSANZ, 2025 key insights, accessed 2026-08-31.
- Roy Morgan reported in August 2026 that 82.5% of Australians aged 14+ ate takeaway food from fast-food outlets in an average six months. McDonald's, KFC and Hungry Jack's were among the largest outlets by customer reach. This supports demand for familiar takeaway categories, but does not establish the economics or differentiation of a new entrant. Source: Roy Morgan, 18 August 2026, accessed 2026-08-31.

## 2. Candidate implications

### Burgers / chicken

**Status:** Strong candidates for deeper costing and delivery testing.

Rationale: strong category familiarity and evidence of substantial Australian takeaway penetration. Main risks are intense competition, commodity-style positioning, delivery quality degradation, cooking throughput, food-safety controls and platform acquisition costs.

### Asian street food

**Status:** Candidate for deeper testing.

Rationale: potential for flavour differentiation, bundles and ingredient overlap. Key unknowns are menu complexity, ingredient sourcing, consistency, prep time and delivery resilience. No score is assigned until recipe and supplier evidence exists.

### Healthy / protein bowls

**Status:** Candidate for deeper testing.

Rationale: potentially strong repeat-purchase and nutritional-positioning opportunities. Key unknowns are ingredient cost, cold-chain/temperature controls, shelf life, perceived value and delivery quality.

### Loaded fries / sides

**Status:** Secondary candidate / possible sub-concept.

Rationale: useful as an add-on or bundle component, but likely weaker as a standalone franchise proposition unless delivery quality and differentiation are proven.

### Dessert-focused

**Status:** Candidate but currently lower priority.

Rationale: potentially attractive margins and bundling, but demand frequency, delivery condition, equipment requirements and daypart concentration require validation.

## 3. Queensland compliance evidence

Queensland states that most food businesses need a local-council food business licence, including businesses that manufacture food and sell unpackaged food by retail. Every licensed food business must have a food safety supervisor. Source: Queensland Government, accessed 2026-08-31.

Queensland's current Food Regulation 2026 commenced 21 August 2026 and replaced the Food Regulation 2016. Exact licence/category requirements must be checked against the final menu, processes and premises. Source: Queensland Legislation, Food Regulation 2026, accessed 2026-08-31.

Specified businesses subject to Standard 3.2.2A may require a certified food safety supervisor, food-handler training and evidence/record-keeping tools. Exact classification must be confirmed for the pilot. Source: Queensland Government, Standard 3.2.2A guidance, accessed 2026-08-31.

## 4. Evidence gaps blocking concept selection

1. Representative menu/SKU designs for at least three candidate families.
2. Current supplier pricing and recipe-level food-cost calculations.
3. Packaging tests after 20–40 minutes.
4. Prep-time and labour observations.
5. Commercial-kitchen/equipment requirements.
6. Local competition and price-positioning evidence.
7. Pilot-region delivery-radius and channel economics.
8. Waste/shelf-life observations.
9. Customer testing or comparable demand evidence at the intended price point.

## 5. Overseer conclusion

Do **not** select a final concept yet. The evidence currently supports prioritising burgers/chicken for immediate costing and delivery tests while retaining Asian street food and healthy/protein bowls as serious alternatives. This is a research priority, not a franchise approval.
