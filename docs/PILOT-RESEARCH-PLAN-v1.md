# Ghost Kitchen Pilot Research Plan

**Status:** Active research plan  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 2026-09-01

## Purpose

Convert the current evidence base into decision-grade evidence for selecting the first Ghost Kitchen pilot. This plan deliberately separates market demand from commercial viability.

## Priority candidate set

1. Burgers / chicken
2. Asian street food
3. Healthy / protein bowls
4. Loaded fries / sides as a bundle component
5. Dessert-focused as a secondary concept

No candidate is approved until the evidence gates are passed.

## Research workstreams

### A. Demand

Capture current evidence for category demand, local competition, price points, order frequency and customer proposition.

### B. Menu economics

For each of at least three candidate families, define representative SKUs and bundles and calculate ingredient, packaging and labour costs.

### C. Delivery resilience

Test product condition at 20, 30 and 40 minutes where practical. Record temperature/safety controls, texture, leakage, presentation and customer acceptability.

### D. Operations

Measure prep steps, cook/assembly time, station requirements, throughput, equipment burden, training complexity and ingredient overlap.

### E. Supply

Identify realistic Australian supplier categories and obtain dated indicative pricing. Record minimums, availability, substitution risk and cold-chain implications where relevant.

### F. Location

Evaluate candidate commercial kitchens against council jurisdiction, licence pathway, equipment, extraction, storage, dispatch access, operating hours and delivery radius.

### G. Channel economics

Model marketplace-heavy, hybrid and direct-order-led scenarios. Include commissions, payment processing, delivery, customer acquisition, discounts and retention effects.

### H. Free-delivery proposition

Test threshold-based, partially subsidised, fully subsidised and customer-funded delivery scenarios. Do not assume free delivery means the business absorbs the full delivery cost.

## Decision matrix

A candidate advances only when it has:

- evidence-backed menu structure
- recipe-level cost assumptions
- plausible AOV/bundle structure
- operationally achievable prep/throughput
- acceptable delivery resilience
- viable kitchen/equipment path
- identifiable supply path
- plausible local positioning
- no unresolved disqualifier
- unit economics that remain viable under conservative assumptions

## Current directional finding

Australian takeaway demand and online ordering are strong enough to justify continued investigation. Roy Morgan reports 82.5% of Australians aged 14+ ate takeaway food from fast-food outlets in an average six months in the year to June 2026; FSANZ reports 32.4% of surveyed Australians ordered restaurant/take-away meals or drinks online in the preceding 30 days. These are market signals, not a forecast for this business.

Burgers/chicken therefore remain the first costing/testing priority, but competition and platform economics make differentiation, delivery quality and direct customer ownership critical.

## Required outputs

1. Candidate evidence pack
2. Three comparable menu/recipe costing packs
3. Delivery test protocol/results
4. Kitchen/location shortlist
5. Channel economics model
6. Preliminary concept scorecard
7. Decision memo recommending selection or explicitly deferring selection

## Guardrail

Do not convert market-size statistics, competitor pricing, platform terms or supplier quotes into guaranteed revenue, margin, franchisee earnings or final commercial terms.
