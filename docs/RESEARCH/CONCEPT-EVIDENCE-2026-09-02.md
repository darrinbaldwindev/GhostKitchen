# Pilot Concept Evidence — 2026-09-02

**Status:** Evidence snapshot — not a concept selection
**Mission:** GK-008

## Current market signals

Recent Australian evidence supports delivery-first food as a credible market hypothesis. Roy Morgan reported in August 2026 that 82.5% of Australians aged 14+ ate takeaway from fast-food outlets in the six months to June 2026; chicken and burger-led brands are prominent among major chains. This supports category demand but does not establish that a new franchise can compete successfully.

## Delivery economics signal

Uber Eats Australia currently publishes materially different merchant economics by channel. Its published marketplace pricing lists 30% for Uber delivery, 16% for self-delivery and 6% for pickup; its direct Webshop lists 25% with Uber delivery. Uber Direct instead uses a per-delivery model with a published base fee of $10 excluding GST plus $1.10/km excluding GST, subject to other applicable fees.

This means the Ghost Kitchen model should not use a single generic "delivery cost" assumption. The economics model needs at least:

- marketplace acquisition + delivery scenario
- direct ordering + Uber Direct scenario
- direct ordering + alternative delivery scenario
- customer-paid delivery scenario
- franchisor/platform-controlled pricing scenario

## Pricing signal

Uber Eats reports that 42% of surveyed Australian online food-delivery consumers regularly check dine-in versus delivery prices, and that surveyed users considered an average 11% delivery markup reasonable. This creates a potential tension between platform costs and customer price sensitivity.

Therefore the template should prioritise high-AOV bundles, clear value architecture and direct ordering/customer ownership rather than relying entirely on marketplace traffic.

## Regulatory signal

Queensland states that licences, registrations and permits depend on business type, activity and location, and businesses must obtain the necessary permissions before trading. Exact food-business requirements therefore remain concept/location-specific and must be validated before launch.

## Concept implications

### Burgers
Strong demand signal and strong bundle potential. Main risks are intense competition, delivery quality after time in transit, and dependence on a crowded category.

### Chicken
Strong demand signal and strong menu/bundle potential. Potentially attractive for repeatability, but frying/extraction/equipment and food-safety controls may increase setup complexity.

### Asian street food
Potentially strong differentiation and ingredient overlap, but menu complexity can become a major training and production risk.

### Healthy/protein bowls
Potentially strong direct-brand positioning and ingredient modularity, but fresh-produce handling, temperature control and delivery texture/quality require validation.

### Loaded fries/sides
Useful as an attachment/bundle component but weak as the sole first concept unless delivery-quality testing proves strong resilience.

### Dessert-focused
Potentially simpler production and strong attachment economics, but may have weaker frequency and meal-occasion demand as a standalone first franchise.

## Provisional direction

No concept is selected yet.

The strongest **research priority** is a compact, high-AOV, operationally simple concept that can survive 20–40 minutes of delivery, share ingredients across menu items and operate through both marketplace and direct-order channels.

The next step is recipe-level modelling and delivery testing for the top 2–3 candidates rather than broad additional market research.
