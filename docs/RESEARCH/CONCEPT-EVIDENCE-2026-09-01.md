# Pilot Concept Evidence — 2026-09-01

**Status:** Evidence snapshot — not a concept selection
**Mission:** GK-008

## Market signal

Food delivery is a meaningful Australian digital food channel. FSANZ's 2025 online-shopping research reports that 32.4% of respondents ordered meals or drinks from a restaurant/take-away online in the prior 30 days. This supports delivery-first demand as a valid market hypothesis, but it does not establish demand for any specific Ghost Kitchen concept.

## Regulatory signal

Queensland states that food-safety regulations apply across production, processing, distribution and sale, with Queensland Health responsible for overall food safety and local councils potentially imposing specific permits/licences. The template's commercial-kitchen assumption is therefore appropriate as a planning boundary, but exact requirements remain concept/location-specific and require professional/local validation.

## Delivery-worker cost signal

The Fair Work Ombudsman states that an Interim On-Demand Delivery Employee-Like Worker Minimum Standards Order applies from 17 August 2026 to covered employee-like workers engaged through digital platforms. The franchise model must therefore avoid treating third-party delivery economics as static and must monitor applicable platform/worker-cost changes.

## Initial concept implications

These signals strengthen the case for a delivery-first template but do not justify selecting burgers, chicken, Asian food, bowls, loaded fries or desserts yet.

The next evidence pass should compare candidate concepts using:

1. delivery quality after 20–40 minutes
2. recipe-level food cost
3. preparation time
4. equipment burden
5. AOV/bundle potential
6. ingredient overlap
7. repeat purchase
8. waste profile
9. local competition/demand
10. training repeatability
